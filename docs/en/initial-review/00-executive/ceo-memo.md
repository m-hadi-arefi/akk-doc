## 1. The Core Executive Questions Answered

### Question 1: What problem do we have today?
Our operations across commercial procurement, foreign currency allocation, customs clearance, central warehousing, vehicle allocation, and dealership warranty settlement are fractured across semi-isolated silos:
- The core financial ledger resides in an aging, closed Delphi-based software (**Tadark**) that lacks open APIs.
- Inter-departmental workflows operate largely via **Shadow IT**: disconnected spreadsheets, messaging groups, physical ink sign-offs, and redundant manual re-keying.
- Conflicting operational data exists: vehicle chassis numbers (VINs), customs duties, and dealer spare parts inventories diverge across units.

### Question 2: How much does this problem cost us?
**Direct Executive Statement:** We refuse to fabricate artificial financial numbers. The exact monetary cost is currently an **UNVERIFIED HYPOTHESIS to be empirically measured during Phase 1 Discovery**.  
However, the verified cost drivers are visible daily:
- Hundreds of professional person-hours spent each month copying data between spreadsheets and Tadark.
- Demurrage risks and clearance cycle delays at ports caused by manual document collation.
- Warranty claim disputes with dealerships and delayed spare parts distribution due to inventory mismatches.
- Severe key-person dependency stemming from reliance on individual spreadsheet owners.

### Question 3: Which problem has the highest priority?
**Priority 1 is Customs Clearance, Logistics Dossier Reconciliation, and VIN Traceability.**  
Every imported vehicle represents substantial working capital. Delays in customs clearance, erroneous tariff calculations, and late VIN allocations directly lock cash flow and delay retail deliveries. Secondary priorities (dealership warranty claims and customer portals) depend strictly on establishing this core data pipeline first.

### Question 4: Do we actually need Artificial Intelligence (AI)?
**For the vast majority of our core transactional operations: NO.**  
A deterministic rule engine, structured relational database tables, clean API connectors, and standardized approval workflows will solve operational bottlenecks at lower cost and near-zero risk.  
**Where AI is strictly justified (with mandatory Human-in-the-Loop):**
- Intelligent document extraction (OCR) for multi-format foreign commercial proformas, packing lists, and customs shipping manifests.
- Computer vision assistance for dealership vehicle intake scratch/damage inspection.

### Question 5: What is the Sourcing Strategy (Build vs. Buy vs. Integrate)?
We adopt a **Governed Hybrid Strategy**:
- **DO NOT Build** an in-house ERP from scratch.
- **DO NOT Buy** a rigid international commercial ERP before our requirements and Iranian tax/customs workflows are codified.
- **DO Build** targeted internal integration adapters, workflow automation engines, and Master Data synchronization hubs.

## 2. Proposed Operating Model Authority
To guarantee execution without administrative friction, the incoming Transformation Leader requests specific delegated authority from the CEO:
1. **Portfolio Pruning:** Authority to pause or terminate low-value digital projects that do not trace to verified operational outcomes.
2. **Architecture & Standards Enforcement:** Authority to mandate technical, integration, and security standards across all internal and vendor-supplied software.
3. **Sourcing Governance:** Final sign-off on all software build vs. buy decisions.
4. **Data Ownership:** Authority to mandate single-ownership rules for vehicle (VIN) and spare parts master records.
